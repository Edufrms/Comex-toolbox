1. The tariff shown is not always the duty you will pay

Market Access Map (MAcMap), provided by the International Trade Centre (ITC), helps research MFN tariffs, trade-agreement preferences, tariff-rate quotas, trade remedies and certain non-tariff measures. The outcome depends on the product, import market, origin, reference date and applicable regime.

MFN tariffA most-favoured-nation reference rate, without assuming bilateral preferential treatment.
Preferential tariffMay be lower when a relevant regime applies and the goods fulfil its product-specific origin requirements.

Customs duty is only one component of import costs. VAT, excise, brokerage, inspections, insurance and transport may also matter. A zero duty does not imply unrestricted market entry.

Operational warning: verify national tariff classification and the current legislation with the importing customs authority before making a binding quotation. This guide is not a customs ruling.

2. Research path: from HS code to market-access conditions

Describe the actual product

Gather commercial name, composition, materials and intended use. A six-digit HS code is a starting point; the destination country may require finer tariff detail.

Select destination and origin country

Set the trade route in Market Access Map. The country the goods ship from is not necessarily their preferential country of origin.

Compare applicable measures

Review MFN and preferential rates, specific duties, quotas and relevant trade remedies, rather than relying on a single headline percentage.

Verify supporting documents

To claim a preference, check product-specific origin rules and the required evidence. Examine relevant non-tariff measures and certification requirements too.

3. Illustrative quotation: a potential €600 duty difference

4. Duty-scenario calculator

Calculate the difference between rates you enter yourself. This is a basic arithmetic exercise, not a live tariff lookup; it excludes other taxes, specific duties and quotas.

If preferential origin cannot be demonstrated, do not budget on the assumption that the lower tariff applies.

5. Four checks before issuing a price

National tariff classification

The destination code can contain more digits than the HS6 heading and may change the applied treatment.

Origin is not shipping location

Dispatching goods from one country does not automatically confer preferential origin there.

Quotas and additional duties

Quotas may be conditional or time-limited; trade remedies can change the effective duty.

Customs duty versus landed cost

Duty is one component. Document customs valuation, indirect taxes and freight separately.

6. Next action after consulting MAcMap

Build a quotation checklist recording classification, origin, destination, treatment, proof, tariff and verification date. Resolve outstanding questions before presenting a final price.
