How to use Trade Map to assess export markets

Turn import tables into a reasoned market shortlist. This guide includes a worked example, a unit-value calculator and a framework for cross-checking statistics.

1. What is Trade Map useful for?

Trade Map, provided by the International Trade Centre (ITC), brings together merchandise trade statistics by product, economy and partner. It helps users identify importing countries, their suppliers, demand trends and the position of a particular exporter in those markets.

It is useful during initial market screening: trade flows help generate commercial hypotheses. They do not replace customer research, distribution analysis, actual selling prices, sanitary requirements or logistics cost calculations.

Questions you can investigate

  • Which countries purchase the product I plan to export?
  • Is imported volume growing, or only its monetary value?
  • Which exporting countries supply the target market?
  • Does my country already export there, and how has that changed?

Access and availability

Visit the official website and check the access conditions applicable to your location and account. Certain features or the legacy interface may require registration or paid access. As the interface evolves, look for equivalent options for imports/exports, product, economy and partners.

2. First analysis: from HS code to market shortlist

Define the product before searching

Find the Harmonized System (HS) code. A more specific classification avoids mixing unlike products. Check the product description and HS revision.

Survey global demand

Select product imports and inspect importing countries. Distinguish import value, volume and trend: large imports do not automatically mean an accessible market.

Open candidate markets

Choose each importing market and see which countries supply it. Examine shares, concentration and the presence of your origin country.

Compare several years

Review the time series rather than drawing conclusions from one year. Higher trade value may reflect higher prices instead of more goods.

Check feasibility

Cross-check tariffs, rules of origin, technical requirements and logistics with specialized tools before prioritizing destinations.

How to repeat this with real data

  1. Search for 080711 — Watermelons, fresh in Trade Map and examine worldwide imports.
  2. Rank markets by value, inspect quantities, and compare changes over available years.
  3. For each candidate destination, review its main suppliers and Costa Rica’s recorded exports to that market.
  4. Export or record the table with the reference year, units and data source. Never present the illustrative figures above as Trade Map results.

Quick unit-value calculator

Enter illustrative values or figures you have verified in a table. The formula uses US dollars and metric tonnes.

Unit value = trade value ÷ tonnes. Example: USD 1,200,000 ÷ 3,000 t = USD 400/t (USD 0.40/kg). It does not adjust for commercial terms, quality or transport costs.

5. Five mistakes that can change your conclusion

Mixing up value and volume

If import value increases but quantity falls, prices may have risen. Compare both indicators.

Not labelling mirror data

When a country does not report trade, Trade Map may use its partners’ declarations. Identify the source and cross-check material discrepancies.

Ignoring HS revisions

HS codes may change between revisions. Check concordances before analyzing a long time series.

Treating unit value as a selling price

Value divided by quantity averages many transactions and is not your product’s destination selling price.

Turning a ranking into a sales plan

The largest importer may have significant barriers, established suppliers or prohibitive entry costs.

6. Market-screening checklist

  • Product description and HS code verified
  • Reference year and units documented
  • Value, quantity and trend compared
  • Supplier mix and origin-country share reviewed
  • Tariffs, requirements and logistics cross-checked
  • Conclusions include data limitations

7. Official sources and next steps

Use ITC documentation to verify definitions, availability and access restrictions. Interface labels may change, but data-quality checks remain essential.
