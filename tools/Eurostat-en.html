Eurostat: official statistics for international market analysis

Use Eurostat to access reliable European Union data on trade, economy, labor, production and sector performance.

1. What is Eurostat?

Eurostat is the statistical office of the European Union. It provides free, official, and comparable data on all EU countries and many neighboring European states.

  • Economy and finance: GDP, inflation, investment, public accounts
  • International trade: exports, imports, trade balance, sectoral flows
  • Labor market: employment, unemployment, wages, productivity
  • Business and innovation: industrial structure, R&D, digitalization
  • Population and society: demographics, education, health, households
Note: Eurostat is one of the most reliable public sources for benchmarking, market mapping, and strategic analysis in Europe.

2. How to access the platform

  1. Visit the official Eurostat portal: ec.europa.eu/eurostat
  2. Use the top navigation or the search bar to look for a topic or indicator.
  3. Click on Database to access tables and downloadable datasets.
  4. From there you can filter by country, period, unit of measure, and product or sector.
Practical tip: If you need quick comparison between countries, start by selecting the country and period filters before moving to more detailed breakdowns.

3. Main statistical categories

Eurostat groups data by thematic categories. The most useful for foreign trade and market planning are:

  • Economy and finance: GDP, inflation, public deficit, investment
  • International trade: intra-EU and extra-EU imports and exports
  • Labour market: unemployment, wages, employment rates
  • Business: structure of firms, productivity, R&D, digital adoption
  • Population and society: demography, education, health and consumption patterns

4. How to run a query

  1. Open Database from the main menu.
  2. Select a subject such as International Trade or Economy and Finance.
  3. Choose the dataset that matches your need, for example “Trade in goods” or “Monthly trade data”.
  4. Apply filters: country, time period, unit, flow (imports/exports), and product.
  5. Click View data and then export if needed to Excel or CSV.

You can visualize the data as tables or charts, which makes it easier to compare markets or countries.

5. Practical example

Example: checking Spanish exports to Germany

  1. Go to Database → International Trade.
  2. Select a dataset such as Monthly trade data (extra and intra-EU).
  3. Set filters: Spain as reporting country, Germany as partner country, flow type “Exports”.
  4. Choose the time range you need, for example the last 24 or 60 months.
  5. Review the data and export it for internal analysis or reporting.

This type of query helps you measure the evolution of a commercial relationship and identify whether it is growing, stable or declining.

6. Final recommendations

  • Use Eurostat for benchmarking, strategic planning, and market analysis.
  • Cross-check with other data sources such as DataComex, UN Comtrade or Statista for deeper context.
  • Build your own indicator matrices to compare countries, sectors and periods.
  • Keep track of annual revisions and methodology changes when comparing data series.
Warning: Numbers from different databases may not always be strictly comparable because of definitions, classification methods or reporting scopes.
