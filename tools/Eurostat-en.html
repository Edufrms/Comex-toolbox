1. Eurostat versus Comext: which data do you need?

Eurostat is the statistical office of the European Union. Its Comext database provides detailed international trade in goods data by reporting economy, partner, product, unit and time period. It is useful for analysing changes in EU and non-EU goods trade.

Aggregated dataUseful for overall exports, imports, monthly or annual movements, and trade balances.
Detailed dataAllow country-pair and product studies using the relevant classifications, including the Combined Nomenclature where applicable.

For a product-specific question, detail matters. A rise in total imports does not show that your particular product is growing. Trade values do not reveal buyer identities, domestic consumption or distributor margins.

Key distinction: Eurostat measures goods flows, not signed contracts, customer demand forecasts or profitability.

2. A reproducible research workflow

Write a precise question

“How did Spanish exports of a specific product to Germany change across the last five complete years?” is more useful than “find data about Germany”.

Open international trade in goods data

Visit Eurostat's official database. Choose detailed trade data if you need a product breakdown; dataset names and controls can change.

Set all relevant dimensions

Reporter: Spain; partner: Germany; flow: exports; defined product classification; annual or monthly periods; consistent value unit.

Download and document

Record the dataset identifier, retrieval date, classification, units and periods. Export in CSV or another available format and save the query for reproducibility.

Interpretation limit: unit value is a statistical ratio, not a transaction-level sales price. Quality, contracts and product mix can matter.

4. Calculator: change across comparable periods

Enter two values from the same series with the same unit and equivalent time spans. Percentage change is not a forecast of market potential.

For volume changes, use quantities in both fields. Never compare a full year to an incomplete reporting period.

5. Five errors that change your interpretation

Mixing units

Euros, thousands of euros, kilograms and tonnes cannot be compared without conversion.

Mixing up reporter and partner

Spanish reported exports to Germany need not precisely match German reported imports from Spain.

Ignoring revisions

Recent observations can be preliminary or revised; record the extraction date and statistical period.

Ignoring classification versions

Product definitions can change across revisions; verify correspondence for long time series.

Equating nominal growth with volume growth

Inspect trade values and quantities separately whenever possible.

6. Document your analysis and cross-check conclusions

Reproducibility matters. Record dataset name and identifier, reporter, partner, product, nomenclature, flow, unit, period, retrieval date and methodological caveats.
