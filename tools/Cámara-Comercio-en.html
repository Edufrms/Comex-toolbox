Spanish Chamber of Commerce: support for internationalization and export

Learn how the Foreign Trade service helps Spanish companies identify markets, train teams, access funding, and improve export readiness.

1. What is the Foreign Trade section of the Spanish Chamber of Commerce?

The Spanish Chamber of Commerce is one of the main public-private institutions supporting companies in international expansion. Its Foreign Trade section offers a broad set of services for Spanish SMEs and larger firms that want to export, diversify markets, or strengthen their commercial positioning abroad.

It is especially useful for companies that need a structured approach to internationalization, rather than ad hoc actions. The Chamber functions as a link between businesses and public support mechanisms, local institutions, European funding lines, and international trade services.

Market discovery

Helps identify target markets and commercial opportunities.

Training and advisory

Offers programs to improve export management and international sales skills.

Support for action

Facilitates access to fairs, B2B meetings, digital marketing plans, and sectorial support.

Important: The Chamber does not replace private consulting or a company’s own commercial strategy. It is a strategic platform, not an export shortcut. It is most useful when combined with market research, export planning, and direct commercial validation.

2. Where to start: access the Foreign Trade service

  1. Visit the official Spanish Chamber of Commerce website: camara.es/comercio-exterior
  2. Browse the sections dedicated to internationalization, export, trade fairs, and business support.
  3. Check whether your local Chamber or territorial Chamber has a dedicated office for foreign trade or international business.
  4. Review the current programs, upcoming calls, and eligible sectors.
Practical advice: Programs vary by region and year. Always check the local Chamber that serves your area and ask whether your business size, sector, and level of export experience fit the available support.

3. Main programs and services to know

These are the most common types of support offered by the Chamber system in Spain:

  • Xpande: Advice and support for companies starting or strengthening internationalization processes.
  • Xpande Digital: Digital export strategy and online positioning, often focused on e-commerce or international digital marketing.
  • InnoXport: Support for innovative or technology-driven companies with international expansion goals.
  • International Promotion Plan (PIP): Assistance for trade fairs, missions, commercial actions, and international visits.
  • Training and workshops: Seminars on export regulations, logistics, pricing, market research, and trade documentation.
  • Advisory for market entry: Guidance on sectors, target countries, legal requirements, and potential barriers to trade.
Important: Some services are free, while others are co-financed or require matching funds from the company. Always verify the exact requirements for each program and whether your firm is eligible.

4. How to choose the right service

Before applying to any program, ask yourself the following:

  • What is the actual objective? (Enter a market, start exporting, improve B2B validity, participate in fairs, support digital strategy...)
  • Which market are we targeting?
  • Do we need training, advisory, or direct commercial support?
  • Do we have an exportable product or service with a clear value proposition?
  • Do we have budget or internal capacity to follow through once support is granted?

Not every program is a good fit. The best use of the Chamber comes when the company has a real market opportunity and is ready to act, not merely looking for a free service without internal commitment.

5. Practical example: using Xpande Digital

Scenario

A Spanish SME in packaging wants to start selling in France and Portugal. The company has a product line suited for the retail channel but lacks knowledge of digital sales channels and international visibility.

  1. The company reviews the Chamber’s current export support programs.
  2. It identifies Xpande Digital as relevant because the goal is online international positioning.
  3. The local Chamber confirms whether the company meets the size, sector, and financial criteria.
  4. It submits a request for assessment and receives a diagnostic or tailored action plan.
  5. The company then uses the support to improve product presentation, content localization, and digital demand generation.

The Chamber supports the process, but the company must still define its market entry strategy and the operational effort required to convert leads into customers.

6. What services are most useful in practice?

  • Market diagnostics: Good for identifying where your company could sell and what gaps exist.
  • Commercial and sectoral training: Helpful when the company is new to international trade.
  • Participation in fairs and missions: Useful for building relationships and testing markets.
  • International digital support: Very relevant for online brands or exporters with a digital channel.
  • Business advice and peer networking: Strong for strategic planning and learning from case studies.
Best use: The Spanish Chamber is most valuable when the company already has a target market, product fit, and a realistic commercial plan. It works best as an accelerator, not as a substitute for strategy.

7. Practical checklist before applying

  • ☐ Confirm whether the Chamber program is currently open.
  • ☐ Check eligibility by size, sector, and export stage.
  • ☐ Prepare a clear export objective or market entry plan.
  • ☐ Identify which country or region you want to target.
  • ☐ Confirm what level of internal team involvement is required.
  • ☐ Ask what documentation must be submitted.
  • ☐ Review whether the program is a one-off support or part of a longer strategic process.
Tip: If the Chamber is simply a source of “general advice” without linking it to your business case, you may be better off combining official support with direct market validation and private advisory.

8. Strengths and limitations

What it does well

  • Bridges public support with export realities.
  • Provides training and strategic guidance for small and medium firms.
  • Connects companies with fairs, workshops, and export events.
  • Helps firms formalize their internationalization plan.

What it does not replace

  • Direct market research on customers, distributors, or buyers.
  • Product adaptation to local regulations and local preferences.
  • Commercial cycles, negotiations, and direct sales execution.
  • Appointing the right professional internal team to manage internationalization.
