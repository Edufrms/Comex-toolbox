1. Purpose and scope

SanctionsKit is a private sanctions-screening and research platform that organises public-source records concerning people, organisations and, where relevant, vessels and other entities. Its tools support investigation of named records, source identity and update evidence.

Initial screening produces potential leads for review, not a universal compliance certificate. Businesses must identify the relevant jurisdictions, restrictions, counterparties and transaction-specific controls.

Limit: SanctionsKit is not OFAC, an EU authority or the United Nations. Confirm relevant restrictions at their official publisher and seek qualified compliance advice where needed.

2. Research workflow

1. Identify the subject and transaction

Use a full legal name and relevant public identifying information. Under your compliance policy, decide whether to screen buyers, intermediaries, owners, banks, carriers or other transaction participants. Avoid entering confidential personal details into a public web search.

2. Choose sources and confirm coverage

Open SanctionsKit public search and review the source coverage page. Financial sanctions, export controls, procurement debarments and politically exposed person registers are not interchangeable.

3. Review candidates and original references

Where a possible match appears, compare names, aliases, identifiers, dates and locations. Record agreements and conflicts in the evidence, the source authority and the kind of restriction involved.

4. Verify and document the outcome

Check the live source published by the relevant authority and note the date, coverage and the reason for each conclusion. A source outage or incomplete screening requires an explicit unresolved status, not a false no-match conclusion.

3. Worked example

Fictional scenario: a Spanish machinery exporter receives an order from a company called Delta Industrial Trading (invented name). A search returns a possible candidate called “Delta Industrial Trade”, but its recorded country and business identifier are different.

4. Understand outcomes

Potential matchA candidate needs review. It is not automatic proof of identity, wrongdoing or a prohibition.
No matchNo candidate met the applied criteria within the successfully checked sources. This is not a clearance of the transaction.
Incomplete or errorA definitive result was not established. Resolve missing source coverage, query problems or system errors before closing.
Source freshnessA source retrieval time is not necessarily the date a restriction legally took effect. Distinguish publication, import and screening times.

A company can also be affected by restrictions involving its owners or controlling parties even if its own legal name does not appear on a selected list. Ownership and control rules require separate review.

6. Risks and sources

Four mistakes to avoid

Treating name similarity as proof

Cross-check identifiers and reliable supporting details, recording the rationale for the outcome.

Calling no-match a clearance

Results are limited by selected lists, inputs, matching methods and time of checking.

Confusing sanctions with other registers

PEP data, debarments and export-control lists can have different legal purposes.

Ignoring owners, goods and end use

Name screening does not replace ownership analysis, export controls, licences or territorial restrictions.
