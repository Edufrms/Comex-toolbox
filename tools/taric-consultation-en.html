1. What the tool covers

TARIC is the European Union's integrated tariff, combining measures relating to the Common Customs Tariff and relevant trade and agricultural legislation. Queries depend on classification, origin country and effective date. Results can include measures beyond the headline ad valorem rate.

Always confirm current legislation and official sources before relying on a tariff or origin outcome.

3. Practical scenario

A Spanish business is importing metal components from a third country. Two seemingly similar parts can fall under different classifications based on composition and function. Before quoting, it checks the full code, origin and date, then reviews any additional trade-remedy duties or document requirements. A generic tariff percentage would not be reliable.

This scenario explains the research method; it does not assert current tariffs or product compliance.

4. Interpret the results

TARIC integrates EU measures, while VAT and some national taxes require relevant national references. Duties can be specific, mixed or conditional on quotas or trade remedies. Binding classification questions belong with the competent customs authority.

The interactive guide is educational, not a customs decision.

5. Common mistakes

Classifying by commercial name alone

Ignoring changes in the effective date

Overlooking anti-dumping measures or quotas

Mistaking customs duty for total landed cost
